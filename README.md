## Findings and Recommendations

### What I found

A little more than half of the drivers in this dataset accepted the coupon they were offered, with an overall acceptance rate of 56.9%. Bar coupons were accepted less often, at 41.2%.

People who already went to bars regularly were much more likely to accept a bar coupon. The acceptance rate was 76.2% for drivers who went to a bar more than three times a month, compared with 37.3% for drivers who went three or fewer times. Drivers over 25 who went to bars more than once a month also accepted bar coupons more often than the rest of the drivers.

The Coffee House results showed a similar pattern. Drivers who visited coffee houses four or more times a month accepted those coupons 67.3% of the time. For less frequent visitors, the rate was 44.6%. Drivers traveling with friends had the highest Coffee House coupon acceptance rate at 59.7%, while drivers traveling alone had the lowest rate at 43.4%.

### Recommendations

The results suggest that coupons work best when they match someone’s normal habits. For example, bar coupons should be aimed at people who already go to bars fairly often. Coffee House coupons should be aimed at frequent coffee house visitors, especially when they are traveling with friends or a partner.

A business could use this information to send fewer random offers and focus on customers who are more likely to actually use the coupon.
